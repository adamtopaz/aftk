Uniform continuity strengthens ordinary continuity by requiring one modulus of closeness to work uniformly across the whole domain.

For metric spaces, this is often phrased as follows.
For every ε > 0 there exists δ > 0 such that for all x and y in the domain, if d(x, y) < δ then d(f x, f y) < ε.

The same δ must work for every pair x and y.
That global uniformity is what distinguishes the notion from pointwise continuity.

Compactness hypotheses frequently imply uniform continuity, and the Heine-Cantor theorem is a standard example.
